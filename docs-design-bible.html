Design bible · v1.0 · shipped build

Radiology
Rush

A match-3 that teaches radiology, built to Pixar warmth and Blizzard polish — and argued down to size by six people who each wanted to cut something different.

6 worlds 30 levels 3 bosses 48 case cards 60 fps target no energy meter

The pitch

§ 01

You are a first-year resident in a hospital where the imaging departments have become worlds. Match glowing modality gems to run the scanner, clear the artifacts, and read the case before the Rogue Cell does. Every world is a different department with its own light, motion and music. Every level teaches one real idea from radiology, and the game never stops to lecture you — the teaching rides on the reward.

Session shape: 90-second levels, three-to-five level sittings, one daily four-minute ritual. The promise: you will get faster, and you will actually know the words.

What the team broke

§ 02 · three rounds

Six people reviewed this before a line of code was written. What survived is everything below; what died is recorded here so it stays dead.

Round one — first read

Creative DirectorCinematics

“Six worlds, six art styles, six music beds is not a world, it's a portfolio. Give me one universe with six weathers. And your hook is the smartest thing here and it's buried — the fantasy is becoming a radiologist, not matching gems.”

ChangedOne material language across all six worlds; the residency rank ladder moved onto the title screen and the map header.
Art DirectorEnvironments & UI

“Video behind a match-3 board is the most common way to make a beautiful game unreadable. Every plate ships dark, low-contrast and empty in the middle, or it doesn't ship. And gems can't be six colours — 8% of your male players can't tell amber from gold.”

ChangedSix colours, six silhouettes, six materials. Every plate is composed with a deliberately empty centre.
Lead Gameplay EngineerRuntime

“Cascades plus particles plus a 1080p video loop plus DOM UI is a frame-rate obituary on a MacBook Air.”

ChangedBoard renders to one canvas, UI stays DOM, particles get a pool and a hard cap, and video gets a kill switch that fires on its own when frame time slips.
UX & Accessibility LeadPlayer experience

“Typing mode is the accessibility problem and the accessibility win. Anything timed and text-based excludes people. It has to be optional, forgiving, and never the only path to a reward.”

ChangedTyping is optional and lossless; the match-3 itself became fully keyboard-playable, which almost nobody does.
RadiologistSubject-matter expert

“Two things get you laughed out of a reading room: gore, and fake clinical certainty. Don't make the boss a tumour. Make the bosses the things that actually ruin a study — motion, metal, and a cell that won't behave.”

ChangedThe three bosses are Motion, Metal and the Rogue Cell. Definitions stay descriptive; nothing in the game diagnoses anything.
Live OpsRetention

“No energy meter. Your player is a student, not a whale — the second you gate their studying behind a timer, they uninstall and feel good about it.”

ChangedNo energy, no session timer, no purchasable anything. Retention comes from knowing more tomorrow than you did today.

Round two — after the first revision

Creative DirectorCinematics

“Your boss fights are health bars with a hat. A boss has to change how I match, not how long I match.”

ChangedEvery boss now rewrites a board rule. The health bar became the timer, not the fight.
Lead Gameplay EngineerRuntime

“Your combo multiplier climbs forever, so the correct strategy is one giant cascade and nothing else.”

ChangedThe multiplier caps at ×8 — and hitting the cap fires OVERREAD: screen lights, audio jumps an octave, next match pays double. The cap became the fireworks.
UX & Accessibility LeadPlayer experience

“You put co-op on one keyboard with two simultaneous cursors. That is a fistfight, not a feature.”

ChangedCo-op became Double Read: alternating turns, shared board, shared moves — and your combo charges your partner's meter, never your own.
RadiologistSubject-matter expert

“Typing is where the actual learning lives. Don't bolt it on as a score bonus — make it the thing that saves you when the board is bad.”

ChangedA correct dictation grants +3 moves and a ×1.5 window. Learning is the strategy, not the tax.

Round three — polish

Creative DirectorCinematics

“The level-complete moment is a number going up. That's the last thing they see before they decide to play again.”

ChangedStars punch in on a rising three-note stinger, the case card flips in, and the XP bar fills after the stars — never during. Never split the eye.
Live OpsRetention

“Your daily ritual has no reason to exist on day 30.”

ChangedMorning Rounds serves the cards you previously missed, first. The daily gets sharper the longer you play, and the streak is cosmetic — it never punishes a missed day.
QA LeadVerification

“Cascades during a boss attack during a typing prompt. You will ship that bug.”

ChangedOne explicit state machine — idle → swap → resolve → cascade → boss → prompt → idle. Input outside idle is dropped, never queued.

How it actually teaches

§ 04
  • Ambient.Every gem, world and enemy is a real concept, used correctly. You learn the vocabulary by handling it for hours.
  • Case Cards.One card per level — term, plain-language definition, real example — awarded at the peak of the win moment, not in a menu.
  • Dictation.A definition appears; you type the term. Correct pays +3 moves and a ×1.5 window. Wrong costs nothing at all.
  • Spaced repetition, hidden.Every card tracks seen and missed counts. Morning Rounds serves missed cards first. The player experiences a daily that gets more relevant; underneath it is a Leitner box.
  • Clinical red lines.No gore, no anatomy beyond organs and scanners, no diagnostic claims. The game teaches language and physics — never practice.

Systems

§ 05

Eight-by-eight board, adjacent swap, match three or more. Moves are the currency — nothing in the default game is timed. Cascades pay a multiplier that climbs per chain link and stops at ×8.

Made byPieceEffect
Match 4 in a lineCollimator BeamClears the full row or column
Match 5 in an L or TContrast Bolus3×3 blast
Match 5 in a lineRecon CoreClears every gem of one modality
Beam + Beam—Full cross
Core + anything—Detonates every copy, then their effects
Core + Core—Clears the board

The three bosses each rewrite a rule rather than adding health, and damage only lands when you match the weakness modality, which is always on screen.

  • The Rogue CellInfects a tile every three moves. Infected tiles can't be matched until a neighbouring match cleans them. Teaches: keep the field clean.
  • The Motion ArtifactBlurs a 3×3 into unmatchable ghosting; only a special piece cuts through. Teaches: save your specials.
  • The Implant GolemArmors tiles in chrome — two hits each. Teaches: plan two moves deep.
  • Critical ReadAt 50% and 20% health the boss staggers and a dictation opens. With typing off, the stagger becomes a free double-damage window instead. Never a dead end.

Accessibility & retention

§ 07 · ship-blocking
  • Full keyboard play.Arrows move the cursor, space picks and swaps, Esc cancels — the whole game, not just the menus.
  • Colourblind glyphs and high contrast.A shape badge on every gem; high contrast dims the plate to 25% and lifts tile contrast.
  • Reduced motion.Honours the system setting automatically: no shake, fewer particles, shorter animations. Nothing flashes above 3 Hz.
  • Readable by choice.Text scaling to 130%, a heavier-letterform toggle, and aria-live announcements for score, moves, objective and boss state.
  • No dark patterns.No energy meter, no session timer, nothing purchasable, and a streak that is cosmetic. The only reason to come back is that tomorrow's Morning Rounds is built from what you got wrong.

Polish pass

§ 07b · round two

The first build was correct. It was not yet exciting. Six things changed, in this order.

  • The tutorial.Eight beats on a live board — welcome, your goal, moves-not-clocks, make this exact swap, chains, power-ups, your kit, go. It spotlights one element at a time, speaks each beat, and during the swap step the board refuses every move except the one being taught. Skippable, replayable, and it never explains two things at once.
  • Combo escalation.A named call at every link: NICE, CRYSTAL CLEAR SCAN, CLEAN READ, DIAGNOSIS UNLOCKED, TEXTBOOK, SPECTACULAR READ, OVERREAD — each with its own banner tier, screen kick, flash and voice line. And SURGE: from the third link the board detonates a free 3×3 burst of its own, so long chains physically snowball instead of merely counting higher.
  • Music that layers with the streak.Four synthesised layers — bass and kick, hats, the hook, counter-melody and claps — entering and leaving only on the bar line, driven by how deep your chain goes. Every world has its own key, mode and tempo.
  • A voice cast.The attending calls your reads; Marco the CT tech, Nurse Rosa and Dr. Kim the resident pop in on big chains with a quick celebration. Every line is captioned on screen, so the entire layer is optional and nothing is lost with sound off.
  • The reward, staged.Stars punch in on the stinger; then the case card turns over — a real 3D flip from a holo-foil back with a gold seal, gloss sweeping across the face; and only then does the XP bar fill. Three stars gilds the card.
  • iPhone.Flick-to-swap at a third of a tile, 44pt minimum targets, safe-area insets, no rubber-band scroll, no zoom-on-focus, audio unlocked on first touch — and haptics where the platform allows them. iOS Safari exposes no web vibration API, so the toggle is honest about doing nothing there.

A chain used to be a number going up. Now it is a call, a kick, a burst the board fires by itself, and a nurse leaning in to tell you it was clean.
